How this works

What this is

A public record of every pick from an NBA and NFL simulation model: what it liked, at what price, and how it turned out. Every pick is posted before the game and graded the next morning, wins and losses alike.

How a game is simulated

  • Each game is played out 4,000 times, possession by possession in the NBA and snap by snap in the NFL, using that day's injury report and projected rotations.
  • From those runs come a projected score, a fair spread, a fair total, each team's win chance, and a range for every player's stats.
  • Those numbers are compared with the sportsbooks' prices. Where the sim and the market disagree by enough, that's a pick.

Reading a game card

  • Big numbers are each team's average score across the sims. The arrow points to the team the sim favors, and the middle shows by how much and how often it wins.
  • Spread and Total put the sim's number next to the market's, with the gap underneath. The sim's numbers here are medians, the line it lands over half the time, so they can differ slightly from the gap between the averages.
  • Out lists players the sim treated as unavailable.
  • Updated for late news: if an injury or lineup change comes in after a game is priced, the game is re-simmed before tip and the card shows the new projections, score and fair lines. Each pick keeps the win chances it was posted with.
  • Open Projected box score for each player's projected stats. After the game, the actual number appears under each projection, green if it beat the projection and red if it fell short.

Bets

Bets are 1 unit each and are only totals and player props. To qualify, a pick has to:

  • beat the market's no-vig probability by at least 6 points on a total or 7 on a prop,
  • clear breakeven at the posted price, and
  • still clear breakeven in the stress test, which reruns the game with every team and player edge cut in half.

Each bet shows three meters. Model and Stress test are the sim's win chance for the pick, and the dark tick is breakeven at the price. Market, no-vig is the book's own view with its margin removed. The book and price shown are the best number available when the game was priced.

Leans

Leans are edges that didn't qualify as bets. They are graded as if 1 unit had been bet, so you can see whether they would have paid. The reason is listed with each one:

  • Side market: spreads and moneylines. In backtesting the model didn't beat the closing spread, so sides are never staked.
  • Over 20-pt cap: a gap that large is treated as more likely a model error than a real edge.
  • Failed stress test or too little over breakeven.
  • Prop limit reached: at most one prop per player, and five per game in the NBA (three in the NFL).

Tags

  • Hold on an NFL pick: when it was priced, the books posted props for a teammate the sim had no role for, so that team's projections were inflated. Wait on it. Hold and Role? picks never get a star.
  • Role? on an NFL over: the sim projects more than 1.33× the line, which often means it gives the player a bigger role than the books do. It is still a pick; check his workload first.
  • ★ Hot on a bet: that bet type (market, stat and side, e.g. "3-pointers unders") has 15+ graded bets and is +9% ROI or better this season, after adjusting for sample size: the record is judged as if 40 breakeven picks were added, so 49–22 counts for more than 9–6.
  • ❄ Cold: 15+ graded and −9% or worse, adjusted the same way. Every ungraded bet also shows its bet type's record, tagged or not.
  • Watch list leans: a lean category (the reason it wasn't bet) that is up units over 30+ graded picks. ★ Top group marks a stat group inside it with 15+ graded and +9% ROI, adjusted the same way.
  • Shop under a bet or lean: another of the books in the feed has a better price at the same line, or a better number (a lower line for an over, a higher one for an under). The edge and the graded result still use the listed price, which comes from the book with the lowest vig at the line most books post.
  • Tags are recalculated after every grading and show only on picks that haven't been graded. They describe the record so far, not a prediction.

Paper parlays

The Parlays view tests whether pairing leans holds up. It is learning only: the tickets are never bet and never count toward the season record or units.

On NBA nights it also tracks long shots: sim same-game parlays (2 to 4 legs), SGP+ (two SGPs) and 3 to 4 leg price-gap tickets, built from every prop the books post. SGPs show a fair price to take at that or longer. Also learning only.

  • Each slate, the leans on props and totals are paired into 2-leg tickets: both legs at the same sportsbook and price, from different games, one leg per player.
  • The sim overrates leans, so each leg's win chance is pulled toward the market's by however much the model has overrated leans before that slate. A ticket needs +10% expected value or better; at most 5 a slate, each lean on one ticket (two before Nov 2, 2026).
  • NFL: overs tagged Role? are left out (since Nov 2, 2026).
  • Tickets are graded from the leans' own results. A void leg drops out and the ticket pays on the other one, as sportsbooks do.
  • Rebuilt from ledger slates were built after the fact from leans priced before tracking began, using only results known at the time. They are shown separately from the tracked record.

Results

Picks show pending until graded from the official box score the morning after. Then they show win, loss or push with units won or lost. Units are figured at the posted price: a win at −110 pays +0.91u, a loss is −1u.

When it updates

Everything runs on a fixed schedule (Eastern time). A new board usually shows up here 15–30 minutes after its run starts. Until then the page keeps showing the last board.

  • NBA picks: weekdays at 2:15 PM. Weekends at 10:55 AM (afternoon games only) and 4:55 PM (the night's games).
  • NBA late-news check: every day at 5:58 PM. Game cards with late injury news get re-simmed projections and an amber note.
  • NBA results: every morning at 9:35 AM.
  • NFL picks: Thursday and Monday games at 11:55 AM that day; Sunday's games on Friday at 4:25 PM.
  • NFL late-news checks: Sunday 11:35 AM and 3:35 PM, and Sunday, Monday and Thursday at 7:35 PM.
  • NFL results: Monday, Tuesday and Friday mornings at 9:50 AM.

Using the page

  • NBA / NFL switches leagues. The buttons show each league's season record.
  • Slate shows one night (or NFL week) of game cards. Pick a date from the row above the cards. The ★ picks box (a sidebar on wide screens) gathers that slate's ★ Hot bets and ★ Top group leans in one list; tap one to jump to its game. On a graded slate it also shows how they did.
  • Parlays shows the paper parlay tickets (learning only). A ticket the pre-game check finds broken (a leg ruled out, a line moved a point or more against it, or the ticket no longer +EV) is pulled before the games: no win or loss, and no replacement.
  • Report is the model's report card: how its win chances compare with the market's and with what actually happened (calibration), how much of its disagreement with the market has paid off (trust weight), closing-line value, the sim's game scores, margins and totals against the finals (next to the market's lines), spread and moneyline leans by how far the sim disagreed with the market, how close the player projections were to the box scores, and the week-by-week trend. It updates after every grading.
  • Ledger lists every pick. Filter by bets or leans, by market, and by lean reason; the record for whatever you've filtered shows above the table. Tap any row to jump to its game card.
  • At the top of the Ledger, the calendar colors each night (each week for the NFL) green if it finished up units, red if down, amber if even, and dashed while results are pending; tap one to open that slate. Units over time charts the running total for all picks and for each market or stat; tap a chip to hide or show its line. Both switch between bets and leans.
  • The record at the top right is for the league you have open; switch it between All picks, Bets and Leans (leans graded as 1u each). Type your own unit size in Unit $ to see that record in dollars; it stays in your browser only. The season tiles and the NBA / NFL buttons follow the same switch. Links to a slate or the ledger can be shared, and the ☽ button switches light and dark mode.
